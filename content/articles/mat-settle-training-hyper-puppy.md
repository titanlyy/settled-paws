Hyper is often a mix of age, genetics, skipped naps, and a house that accidentally pays for zoomies. Mat settle training gives your puppy a **job that pays for calm**: go to a towel or bed, park the body, breathe. It is one of the highest-ROI skills for apartment living, guest manners, and your own nervous system.

Not a substitute for exercise or veterinary care. If restlessness is sudden, extreme, or paired with pain signs, see your vet.

## What “settle” means here

Settle is not a military down-stay under threat. It is a **reinforced choice to relax** on a defined surface. Early reps may look like wiggly downs with treat delivery for any pause. Later, you shape longer chin-downs and real rest.

## Gear

- A distinct mat, towel, or bed that can travel.  
- Soft treats your puppy loves.  
- Optional leash to prevent wandering during early teaching.  
- Quiet room for day-one fluency.

## Step-by-step foundation (days 1–3)

**Day 1 — Make the mat magic**

1. Stand near the mat; click/mark and toss a treat onto it when the puppy glances at or steps toward it.  
2. Feed several treats on the mat in a row.  
3. Toss a treat off the mat to reset; wait for return.  
4. End before frustration—twenty successful clicks beat fifty messy ones.

**Day 2 — Add a down**

Lure a down on the mat if needed, or capture offered downs. Mark and feed between the paws. Release with a cheerful cue (“free!”) and invite off the mat. Never drag the puppy onto it.

**Day 3 — Name it**

When the puppy is offering mat downs freely, add your cue (“mat” / “place”) just before they hop on. Feed for staying down five to fifteen seconds. Release. Short and sweet.

## Building duration without nagging

Use a **variable treat rhythm**: treat, treat, wait two seconds, treat, wait five, treat. If they pop up, calmly reset with a lure—no scolding. Duration grows when the mat predicts a fair paycheck, not a trap.

Aim for one calm minute in a quiet room before adding distractions. Many hyper puppies need concurrent nap scheduling; practice after a sniff walk or play, not at peak witching hour until skills exist.

## Adding real-life distractions

Raise one difficulty at a time:

1. You sitting in a chair nearby  
2. You standing and taking one step  
3. Soft TV noise  
4. Family member walking past  
5. Food prep sounds  
6. Doorbell recording at low volume  
7. Real guest at a distance  

If the puppy blows off the mat, the jump in difficulty was too big. Return to an easier level for easy wins.

## Games that tire the brain—not just the legs

Pair mat work with:

- Hand targets into mat downs  
- “Find it” scatters that end on the mat  
- Chew delivery on the mat after arousal (guests leaving, after tug)

Chewing on the mat teaches that the settle spot is where good things finish.

## Work-from-home and evening protocols

**WFH:** Mat beside your desk; reset every few minutes at first. Use a tether if needed so the puppy cannot practice stealing trash between reps.

**Evening:** After dinner walk, five minutes of mat settle with a stuffed toy while you reset the kitchen. This often cuts biting storms more than another round of chase.

## Guests and meals

Gate the puppy with mat view at first. Cue mat, feed for calm, then allow calm greetings. During human meals, mat at a distance with intermittent treats or a chew—begging shrinks when the mat pays better than the table.

## Adolescent and high-drive dogs

Expect regression when the world gets exciting. Refresh foundations with chicken-level treats. Some dogs settle better on raised cots; others prefer flat mats. Experiment. Herding and working breeds may need more total enrichment so the mat is not the only “off switch” you own.

## Troubleshooting

| Problem | Try |
| --- | --- |
| Refuses mat | Reset to treat magnets; check for slippery mats; rule out pain in downs |
| Offers mat then screams | Duration too long; free more often; add chews |
| Only works with bribes visible | Hide treats; pay after the down; vary rate |
| Ignores cue near door | Practice farther from door; leash assist; lower arousal first |

## 7-day starter plan

- **D1–2:** Mat magic + downs  
- **D3:** Cue + 30-second duration  
- **D4:** Chair distractions  
- **D5:** Kitchen sounds  
- **D6:** Chew on mat after play  
- **D7:** Fake doorbell + mat  

Film a before/after—you will see progress your memory underplays.

## Checklist

- [ ] Dedicated mat chosen  
- [ ] Quiet-room fluency  
- [ ] Cue attached  
- [ ] One-minute duration  
- [ ] Release cue taught  
- [ ] One real-life context (meals or desk) started  
- [ ] Nap schedule supporting calm  

Mat settle turns “hyper” into “has a plan.” Pay for calm like it matters—because in a small apartment, it does.


## Using settle to cut evening chaos

Witching-hour biting often drops when mat settle follows a structured routine: walk → brief tug → mat + chew → lights quieter. You are teaching an off-ramp, not suppressing personality. Keep chews safe and sized for your puppy; supervise anything that can be swallowed.

## Travel mats and cafes

Once home fluency exists, fold a towel mat for patio practice at off-peak times. Sit far from foot traffic. Pay for downs. Leave before your puppy fails. Public settle is a graduate skill—do not start there.

## Family consistency

If one person cues mat and another wrestles on the couch at the same moment, the mat loses. Agree on contexts: meals, doorbells, desk time. Post the cue word on the fridge. Consistency is the real treat budget.

Hyper puppies become settled dogs through thousands of paid calm moments. Make the mat the best seat in the house.


## Capture calm outside formal sessions

Anytime your puppy chooses to flop near you, softly mark and deliver a treat to the nearby mat, then lure them fully on. You are vacuuming up voluntary calm and giving it an address. This “capture” work accelerates formal cueing.

## Avoid the bribe trap long term

Visible bait can become a crutch. Fade to: cue → dog mats → pause → treat from behind your back or a pocket. Sometimes pay with praise and a release to a known toy. Variable reinforcement keeps the behavior strong once fluent.

## Measuring hyper to settled

Once a week, time how long your puppy remains on the mat with light household movement. Celebrate fifteen seconds of real looser muscle tone—not only frozen compliance. Soft eyes and hip shifts into a hip-check rest are better welfare markers than a tense down-stay.

Mat training is quiet power. Use it daily and your hyper puppy learns that calm is an active, paid skill—not the absence of fun.

If your puppy only settles when exhausted to the point of collapse, dial exercise into structured outlets and earlier naps. Settle should feel like relief, not surrender after overwhelm.

Practice the release cue as carefully as the down. A clear “free” keeps settle from feeling like an endless trap and preserves enthusiasm for the next rep.
